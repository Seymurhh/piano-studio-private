Hand posture

Lyrical: flat, long fingers playing deep between the black keys where they fit, the hand further in over the keys and the wrist low, as in slow, singing music. Classical: rounder fingers, nearer the keys' front, for fast passages. Auto changes between them slowly with the music: lyrical below about 4 notes a second in a hand, classical above about 8.

Black keys

As on a piano: the black keys sit a little apart within each group and are slightly narrower, as on a real keyboard, so the white keys between them are wide enough for a fingertip. Evenly spaced: each black key on the line between its two white keys, as this picture had before.

The sparkling powder a key throws up as it is struck (in "Keys + hands", its embers). A little left of the middle is the amount it had before this slider; right, up to about two and a half times as much.

The burst of light as a key is struck: the key flaring in its hand's colour, the light it throws on the keys and hands and, in "Sheet music + hands", a white-hot flash where the key meets the line of light. A little left of the middle is the strength it had before; with none, a played key still glows softly while it is held.

Where a ball lands

Dark energy: each ball is a dark orb with a ring of light around it and a dark-red nucleus that pulses and throws sparks inside.

How bright the two balls are, with the light they throw on the paper and the notes. The middle is the usual brightness.

Now and then the balls race: one runs a little ahead of the music, the other catches it and passes it, and so on for a few turns; then they go level again for 10 to 15 seconds. Each note still lights as its ball lands. Further right, a bigger lead.

Each note crackles with sharp sparks of electricity as its ball lands, and a bolt of current jumps to it from the note before. Works with the powder sparks too.

The balls carry electricity: a crackling tail of current follows each ball through the air, strongest at the ball and fading toward its end. Where a ball lands, the note sparks and a bolt leaps ahead to the next note.

As it is played, each whole note (head, stem, flag and beam) breaks into glowing cracks, lit from inside, yet keeps its shape; the cracks flicker with current.
